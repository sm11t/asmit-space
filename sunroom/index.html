01 · The problem

Renting is broken on both sides.

A typical NYC renter spends weeks tracking listings in spreadsheets, chasing brokers who never reply, and uploading the same documents for every application. A typical small landlord now has to lease their own units, because the FARE Act ended tenant-paid broker fees, and they drown in unqualified inquiries with no tools. The search is not the painful part. The coordination is: scheduling, qualifying, negotiating, verifying, following up.

02 · The gap

The landlord side got AI. The renter side got nothing.

EliseAI handles leasing chat for roughly 1 in 10 US apartments, always for the landlord. Apartment List's AI is built to "overcome renter objections." Zumper's AI scores renters as leads to sell. Every big platform's AI works for whoever pays it, and the renter has never been the customer. Small landlords are locked out too: half of NYC's buildings have 1 to 4 units, and the cheapest real AI leasing tool starts at 50 units and about $1,500 a month.

Nobody anywhere has shipped lease negotiation with agents on both sides. That is the open gap we fill.

03 · The product

One portal for the whole rental.

Listing, touring, qualifying, negotiating, and signing, all in one place. Built around agents that listen, read, and see.

1

The landlord talks. The agent builds the dossier.

A landlord describes their unit in a normal conversation. The agent pulls out the facts renters actually ask about: sun by the hour, real noise, the loose cabinet hinge, why the last tenant left. It saves them into a structured per-unit dossier, and that dossier becomes the brain of this unit's agent. Forms never capture this. A conversation does.

sunroom.nyc/list/onboarding
2

Have documents? Upload them. The system reads them.

Talking is not the only way in. An owner can upload what they already have: the lease, a floor plan, an inspection report, utility bills, house rules. The system understands each document, pulls out the facts, and fills the dossier on its own. It knows a boiler report from a floor plan. The agent then asks only about what is still missing. A few minutes of uploads replace an hour of questions.

4

The renter's agent searches, ranks, and shows its work.

Five minutes of conversation produces structured preferences. The agent ranks every unit against them and explains each trade-off in plain language: how far over budget, what is missing, what it saves you. The renter steers. Nothing is a black box.

5

People approve the moments that matter.

Agents draft. People decide. Every binding action, like sending outreach or accepting a counter-offer, lands in an inbox with the draft, the reasoning, the data used, and the other options considered. You can decide in under fifteen seconds, and the record lasts forever.

sunroom.nyc/dashboard/inbox
6

Trust is checked, not promised.

Before a listing goes live, we check the lister against the city's public deed record (ACRIS) and confirm their identity. No major platform does this today, which is how $65M in scam losses happened. Here, "verified" means the deed says so.

sunroom.nyc/list/verify
7

Negotiation inside signed limits.

Each side sets private limits: a rent ceiling or floor, lease lengths they accept, what they are willing to trade, and an expiry date. The agents can only make offers inside those limits, so a bad deal is impossible by design. A lease binds only when both people sign. In supplier negotiations at Walmart, 75% of companies preferred the bounded bot to a human.

8

The Renter Passport.

Identity, income, and employment are verified once through bank and payroll data. After that, every landlord sees simple yes or no answers: "meets 40x: yes," never the paystub itself. Renters stop re-uploading documents. Landlords stop receiving fake ones. New York law already allows this kind of reusable screening report. Nobody has built it yet.

04 · How it works

Built like infrastructure, not a chatbot.

Every agent action is traced end to end from day one. Every binding action writes a proposal with the evidence behind it, plus an audit log entry. Approvals are atomic, so the same thing can never be approved twice. And both sides run the same model tier, so negotiation is fair by design: nobody wins because their agent was smarter.

1

Onboard by talking

Both sides onboard through conversation. Preferences and dossiers are saved as structured, editable data, never lost in chat history.

2

Verified handshake

Deed-checked landlords. Passport-verified renters. Agents only ever talk to verified people, on the platform, on the record.

3

Negotiation within limits

Signed limits define what each agent is allowed to offer. The system cannot express a deal outside them.

4

People sign the close

Lease draft, e-signature, money. Every binding moment needs a fresh human approval, and both parties get the deal receipt.
