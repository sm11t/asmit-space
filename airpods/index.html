The artifact · US20230225659A1

A patent,
read as a system spec.

US20230225659A1, titled “Biosignal Sensing Device Using Dynamic Selection of Electrodes.” Apple filed it in January 2022 and it was published in July 2023. The inventor is Erdrin Azemi, who is in Apple's Biosignal Intelligence Group. Then in November 2025 Apple published PARS, a self supervised pretraining encoder for ear EEG long range temporal dependencies. The patent and the model paper line up almost too cleanly.

Read as a spec, the design is solving three things at once. Contact variance: over provision 17 candidate electrodes around the silicone tip and let an on bud classifier pick the best 4 or so per user, per moment. Three signals, one geometry:the ear is close to the temporal lobe (EEG), the masseter muscle (EMG), and the eyes (EOG), so one electrode array reads all three. Privacy at the chip: raw EEG never crosses Bluetooth. The bud sends out band powers and state labels, not waveforms.

This page is my engineering read of that. What the patent claims, what the ML stack actually decodes from 2 to 4 channels at the ear, where the physics stops being negotiable, and what the developer surface would feel like.

The signals

Five rhythms.
One ear.

Your brain makes electrical waves all the time. Different rhythms line up with different mental states. Doctors have been measuring them for nearly a century, usually with electrodes glued to the scalp. Apple's patent moves those electrodes inside an earbud. Here is what each rhythm tells you.

δ
Delta
0.5–4 Hz
The slowest brain rhythm. It dominates during deep, dreamless sleep, the kind your body and brain need for repair, hormone release and memory work.
Enables
Sleep stage classification (N3 / slow wave sleep). Recovery debt over time. Slow wave enhancement using timed audio.
θ
Theta
4–8 Hz
Rhythm of drowsiness, dreaming (REM), and also deep focus. Frontal midline theta is the EEG signature of being in flow.
Enables
REM sleep detection, lucid dream cues, flow state detection, drowsy driving warnings.
α
Alpha
8–12 Hz
Relaxed wakefulness. The moment you close your eyes, alpha surges. It is the most reliable validation signal in all of EEG.
Enables
Meditation feedback, stress monitoring, relaxed attention detection, real time neurofeedback.
β
Beta
12–30 Hz
Active thinking, problem solving, sustained attention. High beta also shows up with anxiety, so context matters more than raw amplitude.
Enables
Cognitive load tracking, attention tracking, anxiety screening, ADHD support.
γ
Gamma
30–80 Hz
Fast oscillations linked to binding sensory information into one unified perception. Elevated in expert meditators and in some altered states.
Enables
Advanced meditation feedback, cognitive binding research, altered state detection (with caveats).
EMG
Jaw muscle activity
The masseter, the jaw muscle, sits right next to the ear. Its electrical signal is huge compared to brain activity. That makes silent commands using small jaw clenches the easiest signal on the whole stack.
EOG
Eye movement
When your eyes move, the cornea and retina make a small voltage that nearby electrodes can pick up. The ear is close enough to catch horizontal saccades, which is useful for sleep staging, drowsiness detection, and eye control interfaces.

Why the ear
The ear canal sits closer to the temporal lobe than most of the scalp, it is mechanically stable, it is socially invisible, and you can wear it 24/7. It is also right next to the jaw and the eyes, so the same earbud picks up three signal types at once. No headset has that geometry.

And what no model is going to fix

Six barriers
no model fixes.

The accuracy table above tells you how good things get with the right pipeline. This section is the opposite. These are claims that sound great on a product page and that the physics simply does not support. Each row names the strongest published evidence and the specific reason scaling compute, parameters or training data does not unblock it. If a roadmap promises any of these from an earbud in 2026, it is overclaiming.

Don't claim
Mind reading / thought-to-text
Physics: Compute and model size cannot fix this. The signal isn't there.
Brain2Qwerty 2025: 32% CER (MEG) · 67% CER (EEG)

Meta's Brain2Qwerty paper (Feb 2025) is the best non invasive result anyone has ever published. It needs a magnetically shielded MEG room, costs around $3M, and still has a 32 percent character error rate. With 60 channel lab EEG it is 67 percent. The subjects were even physically typing to generate clean labels. A 2 to 4 channel earbud is several orders of magnitude further away from this. The imagined speech meta analysis (Alzahrani 2024) puts 4 class within subject at 29 to 36 percent, and cross subject is barely above chance at larger vocabularies.

Don't claim
Image / visual content reconstruction
Physics: Volume conduction is irreversible. No transformer fixes this.
MindEye2 needs ~75,000 voxels · EEG offers ~1–4 mm²

MindEye2 reconstructs images from brain activity using fMRI's 50,000 to 90,000 voxels per session. EEG's effective spatial resolution after volume conduction is about 1 to 4 mm squared, which is three to four orders of magnitude coarser. You can do category level retrieval (face vs object, with calibration) at best. Pixel level reconstruction is a physics problem, not a model problem.

Don't claim
Frontal cortex / executive function from the ear
Physics: Geometry. The signal travels millimeters, then is gone.
Zero frontal coverage from in-canal positions

The ear canal sits near the temporal lobe. It is not near the prefrontal cortex, which is where executive function, working memory and decision making live. You cannot do source localization from 2 to 6 temporal area channels. No ICA, no dipole fitting. You can't tell frontal load apart from occipital load. This is not a calibration problem. The cortical signal is not reaching the electrode.

Don't claim
Fine motor imagery without per-user calibration
Physics: Wrong electrodes for the wrong cortex.
Cross-subject zero-shot motor imagery: 60–70%

Motor cortex sits at C3 and C4, top of the head, which is anatomically unreachable from temporal electrodes. Best 2 class motor imagery from ear positions is 70 to 85 percent within subject after a 5 plus minute calibration session. Cross subject zero shot drops to 60 to 70 percent, barely operational. BCI Competition IV results have not fundamentally moved in a decade. Foundation model fine tuning helps, but does not solve it.

Don't claim
High-gamma decoding (>60 Hz)
Physics: Skin-electrode impedance + EMG contamination.
Gamma >30 Hz: noise floor · >60 Hz: lost

High gamma is the most informative non invasive band for fine cognitive tasks, and it is only really available from ECoG (intracranial). With dry in ear electrodes, gamma above 30 Hz is at the noise floor. Above 60 Hz it is gone. Not low SNR. Gone. That is why the most ambitious EEG decoding work happens with surgically implanted grids, not earbuds.

Don't claim
24/7 continuous wear at clinical signal quality
Physics: Battery + biology, not transformers.
Realistic continuous wear: ~8–12 hours

Published dry electrode continuous operation tops out around 40 hours. Hydrogel is stable for around 12 hours. No peer reviewed study has shown stable EEG impedance across a full 24 hour continuous wear period as of April 2026. Cerumen buildup, skin tolerability, motion artifacts, and the case recharge cycle pin realistic wear to around 8 to 12 hours. The 24/7 promise is the hardest engineering problem on this page. It is harder than the sensing itself.

Farwell & Donchin's P300 speller is still the gold standard for non-invasive BCI communication. Thirty-eight years later. That's the actual pace of this field. Anyone selling faster is selling a deck, not a product.

The obvious worry, answered

Privacy
is the architecture.

We just put your brain on a phone screen. The obvious next question is whether any of this is safe to ship. Brain data is the most sensitive class of personal information ever collected at consumer scale. The 2024 to 2026 wave of neural rights legislation did not happen because researchers were nervous. It happened because consumer wearables started shipping. Every architectural decision below is what compliance actually looks like in code.

Regulatory floor
  • · CA SB 1223 (Jan 2025): neural data = sensitive PI
  • · CO HB 24-1058 (Aug 2024): explicit consent, deletion
  • · EU AI Act Art. 5: mental state inference restricted
  • · MIND Act (federal): in committee, 2026
01
SB 1223 · HB 24-1058 · EU AI Act Article 5
On-device inference is the architecture, not the feature
California SB 1223 (Jan 2025) classifies neural data as sensitive personal info with opt-in consent. Colorado HB 24-1058 (Aug 2024) and the federal MIND Act track the same direction. EU AI Act restricts mental-state inference outright. Any architecture that ships raw EEG to the cloud is non-compliant from day one. The on-bud chip extracts band powers, runs the L1 classifier, and emits derived state labels. Raw EEG never crosses Bluetooth.
02
Mirrors HealthKit's clinical-vs-derived split
Three classes of data, three different boundaries
Raw EEG: stays on the bud, never persisted. Derived features (band powers, spectrogram tiles): stay on the iPhone, encrypted at rest. Brain-state labels (drowsy, focused, REM): can sync to HealthKit only with per-purpose consent. The boundary is enforced by the SDK, not by an app developer's good intentions.
03
Same threat model as Face ID enrollment
Brainprints stay as hashed templates
Resting EEG is as identifying as a fingerprint. The auth template is a one-way hashed feature vector stored in the Secure Enclave. There is no replayable signal. There is no centralized template database. The biometric cannot be lifted, and it cannot be used on an unconscious user (which is itself a constraint that needs to be tested, not assumed).
04
FedAvg + DP-SGD; B2B reference: Beacon, brain.space
Federated learning, not centralized collection
Population level model improvements use FedAvg style training. Gradients are computed on device, only the gradient update leaves, and only after differential privacy noise is added. The cloud sees neither raw signals nor labeled state. It only sees aggregate weight deltas. This is roughly how Beacon Biosignals and brain.space describe their pharma grade backends too.
05
Every event ships {value, confidence, windowMs}
Refusal is a first-class behavior
30% of ambulatory windows are motion-corrupted. The pipeline must refuse to score those, surface a low-confidence label, and let the calling app decide whether to act. Silently smoothing bad signal into plausible numbers is the failure mode for every brain-wellness wearable that has lost user trust.
06
SB 1223 §1798.99.31 deletion timelines
One tap deletes everything
Brain Privacy gets its own Settings zone. Per-data-type on-device-vs-cloud toggles. One tap purges the device. A second tap revokes any cloud backup under right-to-deletion (30-day floor under SB 1223). The user does not need to call support.

The anatomy

What's inside
the tip.

Two of the patent figures are cross sections, sliced sideways through the ear tip to show how the electrodes actually sit against your skin. Layer by layer, this is what is in there.

01
Silicone outer shell
Soft, replaceable, conductive silicone tip. The same material as today's AirPods Pro tips, but doped with conductive particles where the electrodes sit.
02
Electrode array
17 active electrodes embedded between the inner and outer silicone layers. Each one is a flat conductive contact a few millimeters across.
03
Compliant foam interior
Foam or memory shape material that expands to fit the ear canal. It pushes the electrodes outward against the skin so they keep clean contact even as you move.
04
Sensor PCB on the housing
Inside the bud body sits the analog front end, the ADC, an H4 class chip running on device ML, and the reference electrodes 210a, b and c.
The architecture

What you'd need
to make it work.

Why seventeen instead of two
Every ear canal is shaped a little differently. A single fixed electrode position works for some users and produces nothing but noise for others. The patent's answer is to over provision and let an AI pick the best subset for each person, every moment.
An AI that picks the cleanest channels
The on bud chip samples all 17 electrodes continuously and composites the 2 to 4 channels with the lowest impedance, the cleanest signal, and the most useful spatial coverage. That is sixteen times redundancy at any given second.
Three signal types, one form factor
EEG (brain), EMG (jaw), and EOG (eyes) all leave electrical footprints near the ear. The same electrodes that read alpha rhythms also read jaw clenches and horizontal eye movements. No other wearable position has this geometry.
The signal stays on the bud
Raw EEG never crosses Bluetooth. The chip extracts band powers, classifies brain state labels, and ships those features to the iPhone. The most sensitive data, the actual waveforms, never leaves the body.

The ML stack

Four layers,
two milliwatts.

Coarse states (sleep stage, drowsy, alpha gating) run on the bud for microjoules of energy. Heavier inference (attention decoding, cognitive load) gets handed off to the iPhone's Neural Engine. Raw EEG never leaves the body. This split is forced by the power budget and the regulation, not by taste.

L0
On bud DSP
ARM Cortex M class DSP, on the earbud
around 1 mW continuous

Notch filter at 50 and 60 Hz, bandpass from 0.5 to 40 Hz, plus IMU gated artifact subspace reconstruction. The 6 axis IMU vetoes motion contaminated windows before they ever reach a model.

IMU assisted ASR cuts compute by about 94% vs standard ASR.

L1
Riemannian + EEGNet
On bud, Snapdragon S7 class NPU (IDUN's validated stack)
36.7 µJ per inference · 7.6 mW total

Tangent space projection of channel covariances plus a logistic regression head, for coarse states like sleep stage, drowsy, alpha gating. EEGNet (2 to 10k params, depthwise separable convolutions) handles slightly heavier classifications. Both are electrode agnostic and survive non stationarity, which is why they keep winning MOABB on low channel data.

MOABB 2024 (arXiv:2404.15319): Riemannian tops a 30 pipeline, 36 dataset benchmark on scarce data EEG.

L2
Foundation model on phone
iPhone Neural Engine / Snapdragon Hexagon
around 50 to 200 ms per inference, phone side only

Channel independent transformer (LaBraM, EEGPT, BIOT) fine tuned on the ear electrode geometry. Used for cognitive load estimation, attention decoding, and multimodal fusion with HRV and IMU. Cross subject generalization still drops 15 to 30 percent, so expect a 5 to 10 minute calibration session per user for the fine grained tasks.

LaBraM Huge has 369M params and 0.814 BAC on TUAB. Channel agnostic tokenization is the part that unlocks ear geometry.

L3
Cloud, opt in only
Off device, encrypted, derived embeddings only
Zero raw EEG ever leaves the bud

Population level model updates, longitudinal trend computation, B2B research APIs (Beacon Biosignals, brain.space template). Regulatory floor: California SB 1223 plus EU AI Act mental state restrictions make on device the default. Cloud is reserved for derived features the user explicitly opts in to.

brain.space coined Brain Data as a Service. Beacon raised $97M Series B for the pharma biomarker backend.

The unintuitive part

Transformers don't win here.
Riemannian does.

The 2024 MOABB benchmark ran 30 pipelines across 36 datasets. On low channel, scarce label EEG (which is exactly the earbud regime), tangent space projection of channel covariances plus a logistic regression is the consistent winner. Three reasons:

01
Covariance matrices live on the SPD manifold. Projecting them into tangent space gives you Euclidean, electrode agnostic features that survive non stationarity.
02
Computationally trivial. Milliseconds on a Cortex M. No pretraining corpus, no GPU, no labeled millions of samples.
03
Foundation models only start to win once you have 1,000+ labeled examples per task and a fine tuned domain encoder. Below that bar, they do not beat Tangent Space plus Logistic Regression.
Pragmatic stack for a 2026 build
# pyriemann + braindecode, the boring stack that ships
from pyriemann.estimation  import Covariances
from pyriemann.tangentspace import TangentSpace
from sklearn.linear_model  import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.pipeline      import make_pipeline

clf = make_pipeline(
    Covariances(estimator="oas"),     # robust to short windows
    TangentSpace(metric="riemann"),
    LogisticRegression(C=1.0, max_iter=1000),
)
clf.fit(X_train, y_train)             # X: (trials, ch, time)

What a BrainKit would inherit

Four design principles every honest
brain-data SDK already follows.

01
Subscribe to labels, not signal
Apps subscribe to derived states (focus, drowsy, REM) instead of raw EEG. Same pattern as HealthKit. Solves both privacy and bandwidth in one move.
02
On-device by default, opt-in cloud
Required by California SB 1223 and EU AI Act, not a feature. The SDK exposes an `allowCloudInference: false` default.
03
Confidence + window + freshness on every emit
Every brain state event arrives as `{ value, confidence, windowMs, computedAt }`. Apps refuse to act below a confidence floor. Refusing is the right behavior here.
04
Calibration is a first-class API
`brain.calibrate({ task: 'motorImagery2Class', minutes: 5 })` returns a per-user adapter the foundation model loads at runtime. Without this, fine-grained tasks plateau at 60–70%.

The white space

Every credible in-ear player is one of: B2B, wrong form factor, two-channel-and-not-a-platform, or Apple's still-unshipped roadmap.

The gap nobody occupies: consumer-first, always-worn, platform-model in-ear EEG with an Oura-style insight subscription and a real third-party SDK on top. If Apple ships AirPods Neural with a BrainKit framework, the category collapses into HealthKit and a startup race for “the brain wellness app on top.” If they don't, IDUN's SDK + a federated training backend is the most plausible alternative path.
